# Business Memo
## To: Product, Marketing & Customer Support Leadership
## From: Data Analytics Team
## Re: Key Findings Before Launching Retention Campaign
## Date: October 2025

---

### Executive Summary

Before any retention campaign is launched, this memo highlights **five critical patterns** identified in the customer data (snapshot: 2025-09-30) that should directly shape campaign design, targeting, and budget allocation. Acting on generic instincts — such as offering discounts to all at-risk customers — risks wasting budget on customers who will churn anyway and on customers who would have retained without any intervention.

---

### 1. Inactivity Is the Strongest Leading Indicator of Churn

Customers who placed their last order more than 90 days before the snapshot date show a churn rate significantly above the overall average. This is the single most actionable signal in the data. However, **the window matters**: customers inactive for 31–60 days are still recoverable with a well-timed nudge, while customers inactive for 180+ days may have already mentally disengaged.

**Recommendation:** Segment the reactivation campaign by recency bucket. Customers in the 61–90 day inactivity window deserve the most urgent and highest-value outreach. Do not treat all inactive customers identically.

---

### 2. Over Half of Customers Are Not Enrolled in the Loyalty Programme — and They Churn More

Approximately 57.8% of customers have no loyalty tier assigned. Analysis shows these non-enrolled customers have a higher churn rate than Silver, Gold, and Platinum members. This is both a retention risk and a missed engagement opportunity.

**Recommendation:** The loyalty enrolment funnel is broken or under-promoted. Before the campaign launches, investigate why the majority of customers are not enrolled. A simple enrolment push (e.g., "Join to earn points on your next order") could reduce churn at near-zero cost.

---

### 3. Web / App Disengagement Precedes Purchase Abandonment

Customers with zero or very low web/app sessions in the 30 days leading up to the snapshot show markedly higher churn rates. This signal is available in real time and can be used to trigger automated outreach **before** the customer stops buying, not after.

**Recommendation:** Connect the web activity data to the CRM. Customers who have not visited in 14+ days and have no recent cart activity should enter an automated re-engagement flow (push notification, personalised email) immediately — not as part of a quarterly campaign batch.

---

### 4. Discount-Dependent Customers Are a Retention Trap

Customers who have historically received very high discounts (average >30–50% off) show above-average churn rates. This challenges the assumption that "giving a discount retains customers." These customers may be deal-seekers who buy only when heavily incentivised and churn the moment the discount stops.

**Recommendation:** Before offering blanket discounts in the retention campaign, identify which customers have a high historical discount dependency. For these segments, consider non-discount interventions instead: loyalty points, free shipping, early access to new launches, or personalised product recommendations. Discount offers should be reserved for customers with strong purchase history who have recently gone quiet.

---

### 5. Unresolved Support Issues Are a Silent Churn Driver

Customers who have raised support tickets with negative sentiment scores — particularly those whose tickets were reopened after initial closure — show higher churn rates than customers with no or positive support interactions. The support team is currently resolving some tickets in under 24 hours, but others are taking up to 74.6 hours. Slow resolution and dissatisfaction are correlated.

**Recommendation:** Before the retention campaign launches, audit the support backlog for customers in the target retention cohort. A customer receiving a retention discount while also having an unresolved complaint is unlikely to respond positively. The support resolution experience must be fixed first for these customers.

---

### What We Recommend Investigating Before Launch

| Priority | Investigation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Why are 57.8% of customers not enrolled in the loyalty programme? |
| 2 | Are there customers currently in the 61–90 day inactivity window who can be reached today? |
| 3 | Which customers in the campaign target list have open or recently reopened support tickets? |
| 4 | What is the true ROI of discount-based campaigns for historically high-discount customers? |
| 5 | Is the web/app activity data feeding into the CRM in real time, or is it being used only in batch? |

---

### Closing Note

The data shows that churn is not random and is not driven by a single factor. The customers most likely to churn are those who are **disengaged across multiple dimensions simultaneously** — low recency, low web activity, no loyalty enrolment, and one or more unresolved support issues. The retention campaign will be most effective when it targets the intersection of these signals rather than using any single threshold in isolation.

---
*This memo is based on analysis of 2,400 customers across 7 datasets with snapshot date 2025-09-30.*
